\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Global Magnetic Field Distribution}
The lunar crustal magnetic field exhibits profound heterogeneity across the surface (Figure 1, \ref{fig:global_mag_map}). At \SI{30}{\kilo\meter} altitude, the field magnitude ranges from essentially \SI{0}{\nano\tesla} over large expanses of the lunar maria to roughly \SI{20}{\nano\tesla} at the most intense magnetic anomalies. The distribution is highly skewed, with the vast majority of the lunar surface experiencing fields below \SI{1}{\nano\tesla}. The most prominent anomalies are concentrated on the lunar far side, particularly within and surrounding the South Pole-Aitken (SPA) basin, and at the antipodes of major impact basins such as Crisium and Imbrium. The Reiner Gamma swirl on the near side represents another significant, localized anomaly.

\subsubsection{Artemis III Candidate Sites}
The 13 candidate landing sites for the Artemis III mission are all clustered in the south polar region. Our analysis reveals that this region is characterized by generally low crustal magnetic fields. At \SI{30}{\kilo\meter} altitude, all candidate sites present field magnitudes below \SI{2}{\nano\tesla}, classifying them as low-field environments. However, localized variation exists within the region, with some sites near the rim of the Shackleton crater exhibiting slightly elevated field strengths compared to the broader polar background.

\subsubsection{Historical and Contemporary Sites}
The Apollo landing sites demonstrate significant variability. While Apollo 11, 12, 14, and 15 are located in extremely low-field regions ($< \SI{0.5}{\nano\tesla}$), Apollo 16, situated in the Descartes Highlands, is located near a moderate crustal anomaly ($\sim\SI{4.2}{\nano\tesla}$). 

Recent uncrewed missions also show varied magnetic environments. The Chang'e 4 landing site within the Von K\'arm\'an crater on the lunar far side is situated relatively close to significant magnetic anomalies associated with the SPA basin, presenting a moderate field environment compared to the near-side mare sites of Chang'e 3 and 5.

Statistical comparison between the high and low field sites highlights the extreme relative difference in the local magnetic environment, although all sites remain strictly hypomagnetic relative to Earth.

\subsection{Biological Synthesis}
Table 2 (\ref{tab:biology_synthesis}) summarizes the major findings from the systematic literature review on biological responses to HMFs. The data indicate pervasive effects across multiple trophic levels and organism categories.

In plant models, HMF exposure has been consistently linked to altered growth kinetics, delayed flowering, and modifications in reactive oxygen species (ROS) metabolism \citep{Agliassa2018, Narayana2018}. Studies on \textit{Arabidopsis thaliana} demonstrate that HMFs can modulate gene expression related to stress responses and cryptochrome-mediated pathways \citep{Xu2012}. 

Microbial communities also exhibit sensitivity to HMFs. Bacterial studies indicate changes in growth rates, antibiotic resistance profiles, and community structure under prolonged HMF conditions \citep{Creanga2009, Zhang2017}. 

In animal models (e.g., \textit{Drosophila melanogaster} and \textit{C. elegans}), HMF exposure has been associated with developmental delays and altered behavior. While direct studies on human health in HMFs are limited, in vitro experiments suggest potential impacts on DNA repair mechanisms and cellular proliferation \citep{Gurfinkel2014, Mo2014}. The radical pair mechanism, involving spin-correlated radical pairs in molecules such as cryptochromes, remains the leading theoretical framework for explaining these non-thermal magnetic field effects \citep{Ritz2000, Hore2016}.
