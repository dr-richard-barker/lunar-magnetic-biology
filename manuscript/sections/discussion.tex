\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Implications of Lunar Magnetic Heterogeneity}
The central finding of this analysis is that while the lunar crustal magnetic field is highly heterogeneous, all candidate and historical landing sites represent profound hypomagnetic field (HMF) environments from a biological perspective. Even the most intense lunar magnetic anomalies, which reach $\sim\SI{20}{\nano\tesla}$ at \SI{30}{\kilo\meter} altitude, are nearly three orders of magnitude weaker than Earth's geomagnetic field (\SI{25}{\micro\tesla} to \SI{65}{\micro\tesla}). Consequently, any biological system deployed to the lunar surface will experience a significant departure from its evolutionary magnetic baseline.

However, the relative differences between sites are not trivial. A site with a local field of \SI{5}{\nano\tesla} may offer marginally more interaction with the solar wind plasma than a site with a \SI{0.1}{\nano\tesla} field, potentially leading to localized variations in the mini-magnetosphere structure \citep{Mitchell2008}. Whether this physical difference translates to a measurable biological effect remains a critical open question.

\subsection{Biological Risks for Surface Operations}
Our synthesis of the literature indicates that prolonged exposure to HMFs poses a multifaceted risk to biological systems. For plant biology experiments—critical for future bioregenerative life support systems—the disruption of circadian rhythms and altered growth patterns observed in HMFs \citep{Maffei2014} could severely impact crop yields. Similarly, HMF-induced shifts in microbiome community structure and increased antibiotic resistance \citep{Zhang2017} present distinct challenges for managing microbial ecology within lunar habitats. 

The implications for human health are more speculative but warrant careful consideration. If HMFs impair DNA repair mechanisms or alter ROS metabolism, as suggested by some in vitro studies \citep{Mo2014}, the hypomagnetic environment of the Moon could act synergistically with the high-radiation space environment to exacerbate cellular damage. 

\subsection{Limitations}
Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the magnetic field magnitudes used in this study are derived from satellite observations at an altitude of \SI{30}{\kilo\meter}. The field strength at the actual lunar surface is expected to be higher due to the proximity to the crustal sources. Some estimates suggest surface fields could reach several hundred nanotesla at strong anomalies. Second, the biological studies reviewed employ varying definitions of HMFs, often utilizing nulling coils on Earth that achieve fields of $\sim\SI{100}{\nano\tesla}$, which is still stronger than the field at most lunar sites. Finally, Earth-based HMF simulations cannot perfectly replicate the complex, multi-stressor lunar environment, which includes reduced gravity and chronic radiation exposure.

\subsection{Recommendations and Future Work}
To mitigate these uncertainties, we propose the following recommendations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{In situ Surface Magnetometry:} Future missions, including Artemis III, must prioritize the deployment of surface magnetometers to characterize the local field at human scale and validate orbital models.
    \item \textbf{Controlled Biological Experiments:} Biological payloads sent to the Moon should include internal magnetic shielding or artificial field generation to isolate the effects of the lunar HMF from radiation and microgravity.
    \item \textbf{Site Selection Considerations:} If a biological payload is highly sensitive to HMFs, mission planners might prioritize landing sites near moderate crustal anomalies to provide even a marginal increase in baseline magnetic field, provided other mission constraints are met.
\end{enumerate}

Future work should focus on integrating these magnetic field maps with detailed models of the lunar radiation environment, regolith properties, and water ice distribution to create comprehensive, multi-variable suitability indices for sustained lunar surface operations.
