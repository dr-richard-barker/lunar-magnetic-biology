\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Data Sources and Processing}
The primary dataset utilized in this study is the lunar crustal magnetic field map generated by \citet{Hood2020} (DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.17189/1520494}{10.17189/1520494}), derived from calibrated vector magnetometer measurements from the Lunar Prospector and SELENE (Kaguya) orbital missions. This dataset provides global equatorial and mid-latitude coverage between \SI{65}{\degree}S and \SI{65}{\degree}N at an approximately \SI{0.5}{\degree} grid resolution, normalized to an altitude of \SI{30}{\kilo\meter}. To accurately evaluate the polar regions critical to the Artemis program, we integrated supplementary polar magnetic field maps (\SI{60}{\degree} to \SI{90}{\degree} latitude) compiled by \citet{Hood2022} (DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.17189/rk57-g992}{10.17189/rk57-g992}) and regional surface anomaly models from \citet{Tsunakawa2015}.

Data processing was performed utilizing custom analysis pipelines in Python. The PDS4 ASCII tables were parsed using the \texttt{pandas} library, and the total crustal magnetic field magnitude ($|\mathbf{B}|$) was computed from the constituent radial ($B_r$), eastward ($B_\phi$), and southward ($B_\theta$) vector components:
\begin{equation}
|\mathbf{B}| = \sqrt{B_r^2 + B_\theta^2 + B_\phi^2}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Landing Site Characterization}
A comprehensive database of lunar landing site coordinates was constructed, encompassing the 13 candidate landing regions for the Artemis III mission \citep{NASA2022}, historical Apollo sites (Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, and 17), and recent robotic missions including Chang'e 3, 4, 5, and Chandrayaan-3. For each coordinate pair, the local crustal magnetic field magnitude at \SI{30}{\kilo\meter} altitude was extracted using bilinear interpolation from the gridded PDS datasets and polar model baselines.

To systematically evaluate the biological implications of these varied magnetic environments, we established a classification scheme based on local field intensity:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High field:} $|\mathbf{B}| > \SI{5}{\nano\tesla}$
    \item \textbf{Moderate field:} $\SI{1}{\nano\tesla} \le |\mathbf{B}| \le \SI{5}{\nano\tesla}$
    \item \textbf{Low/Null field:} $|\mathbf{B}| < \SI{1}{\nano\tesla}$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Literature Review Methodology}
To synthesize empirical knowledge on the biological effects of hypomagnetic fields, a systematic literature review was conducted across PubMed, Web of Science, and Google Scholar databases (2000--2026). The search strategy employed combinations of the keywords ``hypomagnetic field'', ``near-null magnetic field'', and ``zero magnetic field'' with biological terms, including ``Arabidopsis'', ``microbiome'', ``cryptochrome'', ``reactive oxygen species'', ``osteogenesis'', and ``DNA damage''. Studies were prioritized based on empirical rigor, controlled shielding ($< \SI{5}{\micro\tesla}$), and mechanistic documentation.
