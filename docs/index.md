# {{ page.title }}

Welcome to the **Lunar Magnetic Fields & Biology** project. This research investigates the distribution of magnetic field anomalies on the Moon's surface and their potential biological implications for landing site selection and long-term habitats.

### Overview
Unlike Earth, the Moon lacks a global dipole magnetic field. Instead, it has localized crustal magnetic anomalies. These anomalies can interact with the solar wind, creating small-scale protective environments called "mini-magnetospheres." Understanding the strength, distribution, and morphology of these fields is crucial for planning biological experiments and evaluating the biological risks of hypomagnetic environments (HMF) on future lunar missions.

### Key Highlights
* **Crustal Magnetic Heterogeneity:** Extracted from NASA PDS vector magnetometer data (Lunar Prospector & SELENE/Kaguya) at 30 km altitude across 140,000+ points.
* **Landing Site Characterization:** Comprehensive assessment of 23 sites, including 13 Artemis III candidate regions, 6 Apollo landing sites, 3 Chang'e missions, and Chandrayaan-3.
* **Biological Evidence Synthesis:** Systematic review of hypomagnetic field effects on plant development (*Arabidopsis* flowering, auxin transport, ROS), bacterial growth, animal models, and human cellular repair.
* **Interactive 3D Visualization:** Real-time Three.js WebGL globe with dynamic surface heatmap and clickable mission markers.
