#### Abstract
As human exploration returns to the Moon through the Artemis program, the establishment of long-term lunar surface habitats necessitates a thorough understanding of the local radiation environment. This study correlates high-resolution surface magnetic field maps derived from Lunar Prospector and Kaguya data with candidate Artemis landing sites and historical Apollo sites. We find that small-scale "mini-magnetospheres" generated by strong crustal anomalies, such as the Reiner Gamma swirl, may provide highly localized partial shielding against solar wind particles. We discuss the implications of these findings for the placement of biological experiments and future human habitats.
